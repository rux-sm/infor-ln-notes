Session Summary

Sales Order and Warehouse Allocation

Entering a sales order end to end, then following it into warehousing — where outbound advice is generated, and where a partial allocation stops the flow.

Session Date2026-08-04
TypeTraining Session
Orientation only. This is the shareable half of a longer internal record. It carries no citations, and nothing here should be relied on for implementation work without checking it against official Infor documentation.

What this covered

An exploratory session: entering a sales order, looking into how pricing and standard cost work, releasing an order to the warehouse, comparing two different "how much stock is there" views, and a shortage that came up during outbound processing and was never fully explained.

This was an exploration, not a controlled end-to-end run — it stopped partway through, without reaching a confirmed shipment.

Topics

Entering and approving a sales order

What was shown:

  1. Sales ➔ Orders ➔ Order Intake Workbench, opening the Sales Order screen tdsls4100m900.
  2. Entered: business partner BP4000040, order type N10, sales office SL1001, site CSADNA01, warehouse ADNA02, item 100200, quantity 20, price 200 USD per unit.
  3. Order status moved from Free to In Process.

These are demonstration values from this session, not defaults to reuse elsewhere. Where the order type itself defaults from, in general: the customer record first, and if the customer has none set, the current user's sales profile.

Pricing and cost — two different things

What was shown:

  1. Personalization on Sales Orders Intake, exposing hidden fields (status bar named tdsls4100m100).
  2. Common ➔ Pricing ➔ Prices ➔ Price Books — Price Book tdpcg0611m000 with effective/expiry-dated records.
  3. Item Standard Costs ticpr3501m000 — effective-dated cost records for the item.

The discussion connected a sales-order line's price to Price Book Lines tdpcg0131m000 and distinguished sales price from standard cost — related but separate figures. The exact rule for which price-book record wins at a given order timestamp wasn't pinned down in this session.

Planning availability vs. actual warehouse stock

What was shown:

  1. Item Order Plan cprrp0520m000 — item 100200: inventory on hand, demand/supply transactions, Planned Available, and ATP.
  2. Warehouse - Item Inventory whwmd2515m000 — the same item broken out by warehouse row, each with its own on-hand, on-order, allocated, blocked, and economic-stock figures.

The point: a healthy planning-level number does not guarantee the *specific* ship-from warehouse on the order actually has the stock. Always check both — planning tells you the forecast picture, the warehouse screen tells you what's physically there.

What ATP actually is, precisely: for any given period, it's the smaller of two numbers — Planned Available, and total demand — looked at across that period *and every period after it*. A shortage three months out can pull today's ATP down. It's also worth knowing that this planning-screen ATP figure is explicitly not guaranteed to match the number a real sales order gets checked against at entry — that's a different, separate calculation.

What was decided

Nothing was formally decided about configuration. The one real outcome: keep this recording as background material for a future "what happens when there's a shortage" scenario — it is not a substitute for the standard, verified Ship from Stock procedure.

Key takeaways

  • Planning availability and warehouse stock are different questions. Check the item's actual on-hand at the specific ship-from warehouse, not just its planning balance.
  • ATP looks forward, not just at today. It's the minimum available quantity across the current period and every future one — a future shortage can drag it down now.
  • Order type on a sales order defaults from the customer, then the user's sales profile — trace it there if a default looks wrong.
  • The 5-of-20 partial-advice shortage on order 100000636 was never explained — treat it as an open question, not a demonstrated cause, until it's rerun properly.
  • This order type's manual steps are Generate Outbound Advice, Picking List, and Freeze/Confirm; the rest run automatically.
  • Don't trust an existing draft guide just because it exists — this one mixed fact with narration and described steps that never happened.
  • Closing advice from the session: start broad, then rerun and refine once you understand the process — don't try to nail every detail on the first pass.
